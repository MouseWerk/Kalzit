# Kalzit 0.4.0

## Improved

- AI, off until a server administrator turns it on: under Settings > AI (Verwaltung > KI) connect
  a provider (any OpenAI-compatible server such as Ollama, LiteLLM or OpenAI, or Anthropic), test
  the connection and turn AI on. Without a tested provider the switch stays off, and while AI is
  off no AI button or hint appears anywhere.
- The AI page shows the providers with where their data goes, the usage per day and organization
  in calls and tokens, the limits per person and organization, and the text "Which data leaves the
  server" with a paragraph of your own.
- Organizations can turn AI off for their members, connect a provider of their own when the
  server allows it, and lower the limits (Settings > AI of the organization).
- Data sources have an AI part: "AI may read" (off by default) decides whether AI sees the
  source's schema and results, and "Excluded fields" keeps the values of named fields from ever
  reaching a provider. The data source list marks opened sources.
- Before the first AI call, everyone sees once per provider which data goes where.
- Explore: "Explain" explains the query in the editor, "Draft" writes a query from a description.
  A draft is never run by itself: "Apply" puts it into the editor, and running it checks it like
  any query.
- "Summarize" in Explore and in a panel's menu summarizes a result from sources opened to AI, and
  says what was sent; viewers can summarize the panels they see.
- Answers appear while they are written, can be stopped with Stop or Escape, and say when a limit
  is reached or a source is not open to AI.
- "Ask" (Fragen, in beta): ask questions about your data in plain words. The assistant looks up
  the sources, reads their schema, runs read-only queries with your rights, or the panels of
  dashboards you may read, and shows every step with its query and result, the tokens it used,
  and "Open in Explore" for each query. Choose which sources it may use; only sources open to AI
  can be chosen. Chats are visible to you only, listed under Ask > History with the days until
  they are deleted (30 days by default, shorter if your organization says so), and can be
  deleted one by one or all at once. Without a connected AI provider, Ask does not appear.
- Ask can take a dashboard as context ("Dashboard as context", or "Open in Ask" in a panel's
  menu), and a summary offers "Continue in Ask". Under an answer, "Save as panel", "Add to
  dashboard" and "Adjust query" take its query on; a result over time switches between table and
  time series, and each query step names the table it read. The side pane shows the provider's
  endpoint, whether it passed its test and whether data leaves your network.
- Settings > AI (Verwaltung > KI): with several providers, choose which one explains and drafts,
  which one summarizes and which one answers in Ask.
- Data source plugins: server administrators install a plugin from its package file, approve the
  network targets it declares, and enable, disable, restart or remove it. Its kind then appears
  next to the built-in kinds, with the labels from its manifest.
- Every package is checked before anything in it runs: only the files its manifest lists, no
  links, a valid signature from a trusted key. Unsigned plugins only with an explicit switch, off
  by default; a broken signature is always refused.
- Administrators manage the publisher keys they trust. Removing a key stops the plugins signed
  with it, after a confirmation; publishers can hand a plugin over to a new key.
- Updates of a plugin stay with its key and never go back to an older version. An update that
  wants to reach new targets waits for approval while the installed version keeps running.
- Plugin processes start when they are first needed, are restarted with growing waits after a
  crash, and stop after repeated failures until an administrator restarts them. A crashing
  plugin fails only the queries of its own kind.
- Plugin processes run in a sandbox: on Windows with a restricted token in a job object, on Linux
  with Landlock, seccomp and namespaces where the system offers them. The plugin page lists which
  layers are active.
- Plugins have no network of their own: they reach only the targets their manifest declares, and
  only through Kalzit, which checks every connection against the data source's settings and the
  target rules and never connects to Kalzit itself or to cloud metadata addresses. On Windows a
  plugin runs in an AppContainer without network access, on Linux it cannot open network sockets.
  Refused connections appear in the plugin's log and the audit log.
- On Windows plugins do not start while one of Kalzit's files with secrets (`kalzit.toml` with a
  password, the TLS key) is readable by them; the plugin page names the file. A file other local
  accounts may read is shown as a warning there.
- Organization administrators can block an installed plugin for their organization, under
  Settings > Plugins of the organization (Plugins der Organisation).
- Settings > Plugins (Verwaltung > Plugins) shows the installed plugins with publisher, signature
  and key, version, waiting updates, network targets and status, with filters for problems and
  updates.
- "Install from file" shows the checks of the package step by step from the server's answer
  (signature, checksums, platform, Kalzit version), then the network targets to grant; an update
  marks the targets it adds. A refused package names the step and the reason, and an untrusted
  publisher leads straight to adding and comparing its key.
- Each plugin has its own page: its manifest, the granted targets, the process with its sandbox
  layers, its log, the data sources that use it in every organization, approve, restart, turn on
  or off, and remove with a note on the sources that stay. A crashed plugin says when it starts
  again.
- "Signature and trust" lists the trusted keys with their fingerprints. Adding a key shows its
  fingerprint to compare with the publisher's; removing one names the plugins it stops.
  Allowing unsigned plugins, and on Linux running without Landlock, asks for the server's name,
  and a band stays on every plugin page while unsigned plugins are allowed.
- "Add data source" lists plugin kinds in a group of their own with a neutral publisher badge
  and uses the labels of the plugin's manifest; the data source list marks plugin sources.
  Explore and the panel editor take a plugin's query as JSON.
- Installing, approving, enabling, removing, blocking and every change of trust are recorded in
  the audit log.
- Packages signed with Kalzit's own plugin key install without adding a key; the release comes
  with the example plugin "CSV and JSON over HTTP" (`dev.kalzit.csv-http`), which reads a CSV or
  JSON file from a URL into a table.
- Plugin authors write data source plugins in Rust with the Kalzit plugin SDK, and package and
  sign them with the `kalzit-plugin` tool; the author guide walks through it. The message
  interface for panel plugins is published, with an example KPI tile ready for when Kalzit hosts
  panel plugins.

## Fixed

- Test connection and the schema of a data source whose kind is not available answer
  "kind not available" instead of an internal error.

## Checklist for Windows (manual)

- AI on Windows: run Ollama (or another OpenAI-compatible server) on the same machine, add it under
  Settings > AI with `http://localhost:11434/v1` and a pulled model, press "Test connection" and
  check that the answer and the time appear; turn "AI functions" on.
- Open a SQLite source to AI on its page ("AI may read"), add `email` to "Excluded fields", then
  in Explore run `SELECT * FROM customers LIMIT 5` on it, press "Summarize" and check that the
  notice appears once, that the summary streams in, and that the note says how many rows were
  sent; press "Explain" and "Draft", apply a draft and check that Ctrl+Z takes it back.
- Stop a running answer with Escape and check that the audit log shows the call as cancelled,
  without any query text.
- With Ollama connected and a SQLite source open to AI, open "Ask", choose the source, ask "How
  many customers are there?" and check that the steps (sources, schema, query) appear with the
  query and its result, that the answer names the number, and that "Open in Explore" opens the
  query in Explore without running it. Restart the Windows service and check that the chat is
  still listed under Ask > History; delete it there.
- With two providers, choose another one for Ask under Settings > AI, ask in Ask and check that
  the notice names that provider and that the side pane shows its endpoint and status.
- On a Windows service installation, install a signed data source plugin: add the publisher key,
  upload the package, approve and enable it; create a data source of its kind and run a query.
  Check in Task Manager that the plugin process runs under the service account and ends when the
  plugin is disabled and when the service stops.
- Upload the same package with one file changed and check that it is refused before anything
  runs.
- With the plugin running under the service account, check on its page that the sandbox lists
  `app_container` and `network`, that "Test connection" and a query of its data source work
  (through Kalzit's connector), and that setting the source's host to `169.254.169.254` is
  refused when saving.
- Install `dev.kalzit.csv-http` from the release assets on a Windows service installation without
  adding a key: approve and enable it, create a source of its kind with the URL of a CSV file on
  the network, press "Test connection" and run a query; check that the columns and their types
  appear and that the plugin's log shows no errors.
- In Settings > Plugins, install the package with "Install from file", check that the steps,
  the publisher and the key id appear, grant the permissions with "Start after installing", and
  check that the plugin shows "Running" after the first query; open its page and check the log,
  the sandbox layers (restricted token, low integrity, job object) and "Used in".
- Turn "Allow unsigned plugins" on (type the server's name), check the band on the plugin pages,
  and turn it off from the band.

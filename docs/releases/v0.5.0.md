# Kalzit 0.5.0

Single sign-on with OpenID Connect, SAML and LDAP, SCIM provisioning, two-factor sign-in, service
accounts, mail and invitations, several servers on one PostgreSQL database and usage insights. The
Windows installer and service, the deb and rpm packages, HTTPS certificates through ACME, the query
cache and the ClickHouse, InfluxDB and Loki sources shipped with 0.4.2; see its notes.

## Improved

- Single sign-on with OpenID Connect (Keycloak, Authentik, Entra ID and others): under
  Administration > Sign-in (Verwaltung > Anmeldung), add a provider, test the connection and a
  test sign-in, then save. The sign-in page shows "Sign in with <provider>" first; accounts are
  created at the first sign-in.
- Single sign-on with SAML 2.0 (AD FS, Entra ID, Shibboleth, Keycloak and others): add a SAML
  provider with its metadata URL or an uploaded metadata file, test it, then save. Kalzit shows its
  entity ID and ACS URL to enter at the provider and serves its own metadata at a fixed address.
- SAML answers must be signed with the provider's certificate that Kalzit pinned when you saved; a
  second certificate can be pinned for a certificate change. Answers that are unsigned, meant for
  another service, expired, used before or not requested by Kalzit are refused, and the test
  sign-in says why, for example when the provider's clock is off.
- LDAP and Active Directory sign-in: add a directory with its server (LDAPS or StartTLS), a service
  account, the base DN and the login name (`sAMAccountName`, `userPrincipalName` or email for
  Active Directory), test the connection with a sample person, then save. People sign in with
  their directory name and password in the usual form ("Email or username"); their account is
  created at the first sign-in, nested Active Directory groups can be followed, and Kalzit's own
  second factor applies. The service account's password is stored encrypted and never shown
  again.
- Group mapping: the groups an OpenID Connect, SAML or LDAP provider sends, and SCIM groups, give a
  role in an organization and a team. The highest role wins; "IdP decides" keeps role and
  membership in step with the provider and locks the role in Members, "Kalzit adds" gives a
  minimum role. People without a matching group get a default role or are refused.
- Directory sync: every night (or every 6 hours) Kalzit locks the accounts of people who left the
  directory, are disabled there or are in no mapped group any more, and unlocks them when they are
  back. It never deletes an account. When a sync would lock more than 10 % of the accounts
  (adjustable), it stops before changing anything, mails the server admins and shows the list;
  "Apply anyway" asks for your second factor.
- Password sign-in can be turned off for everyone except a break-glass admin, who signs in on the
  unlinked page /anmelden/lokal with a second factor; every such sign-in is in the audit log and
  mailed to the server admins. Organizations can turn password sign-in off for their members.
- SCIM provisioning: Entra ID, Okta and other directories create, change and deactivate the people
  and groups of an organization. Under Administration > Service accounts (Verwaltung >
  Dienstkonten), the SCIM card shows the endpoint to copy, creates, replaces and revokes the
  organization's one SCIM token (shown once, expires like API tokens), turns SCIM on or off, and
  shows what it did today, how many of its groups are mapped and the last error, with a link to
  the audit log.
- Nothing is ever deleted through SCIM: a deactivation or a DELETE locks an account SCIM made and
  still manages, or ends the membership in the organization for anyone else, so dashboards and the
  audit log stay. Owners, server admins, the break-glass admin and service accounts are never
  deactivated or changed by SCIM, and an account that joined another organization or whose owner
  confirmed the address is no longer renamed or locked by it.
- People SCIM creates can sign in at once through a provider with the same address (Entra ID SCIM
  with Entra ID sign-in, for example). Their address otherwise stays unconfirmed: they cannot be
  added to another organization by it until they confirm it, and a mailed invitation from another
  organization lets them take the account over.
- Two-factor sign-in: protect your account with passkeys (fingerprint, face, device PIN or a
  security key), an authenticator app (six-digit codes) and ten recovery codes, under Settings >
  Sign-in and security. After the password or a directory, Kalzit asks for one of them; nothing of
  the app opens before. Removing a method, new recovery codes and a new password ask for a fresh
  confirmation.
- Server admins can require a second factor for admins or for everyone (Settings > Sign-in), and
  choose the allowed methods; organizations can be stricter for their members. Whoever must have
  one and has none sets it up at the next sign-in, in three steps.
- Five wrong codes lock the second step of an account for 15 minutes; members and users show each
  person's methods, a missing second factor and a running lockout.
- Admins can reset another person's second factor after confirming with their own; the reset signs
  the person out everywhere and is recorded in the audit log.
- Recent sign-ins: Settings > Sign-in and security lists your last sign-ins with time, method
  (password, break-glass, OpenID Connect, SAML or LDAP with the provider's name), second factor,
  browser and address, marks the one of this session and shows refused attempts with the reason
  (wrong password or code, account locked, refused by the provider). Server admins see anyone's
  under Administration > Users. They are kept as long as the audit log.
- Service accounts under Administration > Service accounts (Verwaltung > Dienstkonten): accounts
  for scripts and other systems, each with a role in its organization (never owner), and API
  tokens for them (`Authorization: Bearer kz_sa_...`). A token is shown once, stored only as a
  hash, expires after 90 days by default and after a year at the latest, can be limited to IP
  addresses and networks, and can be revoked at once. A token works only in its organization, with
  its account's role, and every created, revoked or refused token is in the audit log.
- Mail server: under Settings > Email (Verwaltung > E-Mail) enter server, port, STARTTLS or TLS,
  user and password and the sender, send a test mail, then save. The test mail shows what the
  server answered, the TLS version and until when its certificate is valid; a problem is shown at
  its field. The password is stored encrypted and never shown again. `[smtp]` in `kalzit.toml` or
  `KALZIT_SMTP_*` can set the mail server instead.
- Invitations: Members has **Invite** for one or several email addresses with a role and an
  optional team. With a mail server the people get a mail in their language; without one you get
  the links to pass on. Links work for seven days and once.
- Open invitations are listed above the members with role, team, who invited, until when they are
  valid and whether the mail was delivered or refused (with the mail server's answer); send a mail
  again (a new link, seven more days), revoke it, or copy a link to pass on again.
- Accepting an invitation: the link opens a page with the organization, role and team; a new
  person chooses a name and password and starts right in the organization, someone with an account
  signs in and joins.
- An account made from a link an admin passed on starts with its email address unconfirmed;
  Profile offers to confirm it by mail, with a link that only works signed in as that account.
  Until then it cannot be added to another organization by its address.
- Forgot password: the sign-in page leads to **Reset password**, which mails a link that works for
  60 minutes and once. The page answers the same whether or not an account has the address. The
  new password ends all other sessions; the second factor stays as it is. Admins can also send a
  member the link from the member's menu.
- Mails and links need the server's public address (`server.public_url`); without it Kalzit says so
  instead of sending links that point nowhere.
- Several Kalzit servers on one PostgreSQL database: run two or more behind a load balancer, so one
  can stop while the others keep answering. Sticky sessions are not needed. Settings, rights, data
  sources, plugins and certificates changed on one server reach the others within about a second;
  a session ended on one server is refused on all of them at once.
- One server leads and runs what must run once: retention cleanups, the plugin catalog refresh,
  the daily update check and HTTPS certificate orders. When it stops, another server takes over
  within seconds; after a crash within 20 seconds.
- Verwaltung > Instanzen (Administration > Instances) lists the servers with their host, version,
  start, last heartbeat and which one leads, and warns while they run different versions.
- With several servers, a certificate from Let's Encrypt or another ACME authority is ordered once
  and served by every server, and plugins installed through one server run on all of them.
- With several servers, Kalzit does not install updates on its own; Updates says so. Update one
  server after the other with its package; the operations guide
  (docs/operations/high-availability.md) describes the load balancer, health checks and rolling
  updates.
- A second Kalzit server started on the same SQLite database now stops at once with a clear
  message instead of running next to the first.
- Usage insights under Administration > Usage (Verwaltung > Nutzung): the most viewed dashboards,
  the slowest panels (median and 95th percentile), panels and data sources with errors, the cache
  hit rate per data source, dashboards nobody opened in 30 or 90 days, and the trend over the last
  24 hours, 7, 30 or 90 days. Every row links to its dashboard, panel or data source.
- Usage is counted per dashboard, panel and data source only, never per person: no user, session
  or address is stored for it. Organization admins see their organization, server admins every
  organization or one.
- Settings > Storage and retention: turn "Record usage" off (applies at once, with undo) and choose
  how long usage counts are kept (90 days at most, the default).
- Hierarchy panels can use ELK (the Eclipse Layout Kernel) instead of Kalzit's own layered layout:
  under Layout in the panel options, pick "ELK layered" and set the direction, node and layer
  spacing and the edge routing. Kalzit's layout stays the default; ELK loads only when a panel uses
  it, gives the same result the same layout every time, and if it cannot run the panel shows
  Kalzit's layout and says so.
- The Windows installer's wizard speaks German on a Windows with German as its display language
  and English otherwise, from the same MSI file; `TRANSFORMS=:1031` on the msiexec command line
  chooses German on any Windows.

## Fixed

- A data source whose database is down now says so within a few seconds, naming the address and
  the cause ("Keine Verbindung zu 127.0.0.1:15432: Verbindung abgelehnt"), instead of a pool
  timeout after 10 seconds. A server that does not answer at all is reported after the connect
  timeout with the address. This applies to PostgreSQL, MySQL and MariaDB, SQL Server, Neo4j,
  Memgraph and a single MongoDB server; sources reached over HTTP (Prometheus, Elasticsearch,
  ClickHouse and others) name a refused address or a host name that does not resolve the same way,
  and a TLS failure says what failed instead of only "error sending request".
- After a database restarted, the first query no longer fails with "early eof": connections that
  broke while they waited are noticed and replaced, and a query that hits one runs once more on a
  fresh connection. When a connection does break during a query, the panel says that the
  connection was interrupted; the driver's own text is under Inspect.
- An unexpected error inside one request no longer drops the connection or affects later
  requests: the request answers with the usual error message and its request id, and the server
  log has the cause under the same id.
- Clients can no longer tie up the server with connections that do nothing: the head of a request
  must arrive within 30 seconds of its first byte, and a connection without a request in progress
  is closed after 75 seconds (an HTTP/1 connection waiting for its next request, an HTTP/2
  connection without an open stream, or one that never sent anything). Live updates and answers
  still being streamed are not cut. At most 4,096 connections are served at once, and at most 256
  from one client address; connections from the same computer and from the reverse proxies in
  `security.trusted_proxies` do not count against that. Behind a proxy that reuses connections,
  keep its idle timeout below 75 seconds (see the HTTPS guide).
- A request that takes longer than two minutes answers "The server is busy right now" instead of
  waiting forever. Queries, data source tests, exports, live updates and AI answers keep their own
  time limits; update and plugin uploads, update downloads, installing or rolling back an update
  and backups are not cut by it.
- Long values in the settings' lists, such as a long time zone, are no longer cut off without a way
  to read them: the list grows to a sensible width, ends a longer value in "..." and shows it whole
  when you point at it.
- The update channel, the daily check and the maintenance window are set in one place, the Updates
  page, and apply at once with undo like every other setting; Settings > Updates links there, and
  searching the settings for one of them opens the Updates page at that setting.
- A setting from `kalzit.toml` or the environment shows its control turned off with a short
  "locked" badge and a line that says where it is set, and offers no undo: in the settings sections
  (the mail server, the sign-in page texts, AI and the plugin catalog included) and on the Updates
  page. A search for such a setting still jumps to its row.

## Checklist for Windows (manual)

- Two-factor sign-in with Windows Hello: open Kalzit on Windows 11 by its host name (not an IP
  address), add a passkey under Settings > Sign-in and security with Windows Hello, sign out and
  sign in again with the passkey. Repeat with an authenticator app on a phone.
- Recent sign-ins: after signing in with the passkey, check that Settings > Sign-in and security
  lists the sign-in with "Passkey", "Edge on Windows" (or your browser) and "This session"; sign in
  once with a wrong password and check that the refused attempt appears. As a server admin, open
  Verwaltung > Benutzer, choose "Letzte Anmeldungen" for that account and check the same list.
- Installer wizard: run the MSI on a fresh Windows 11 and on Windows Server 2025, keep the
  defaults, and check that `http://localhost:3000` opens, that `setup-token.txt` in the data
  folder holds the setup code, and that the service "Kalzit" runs as `NT SERVICE\Kalzit`.
- Wizard settings: install with another port, a data folder on another drive, "Self-signed
  certificate" and the firewall ticked; check that HTTPS answers on 3443 (with the browser's
  warning), that Windows Defender Firewall has the inbound rule "Kalzit", and that the data
  folder's Security tab lists only Administrators and Kalzit.
- German wizard: on a Windows with German as its display language, run the MSI and check that
  every page is German (Servereinstellungen with HTTP-Port, Datenordner, HTTPS and "Port(s) in der
  Windows Defender Firewall öffnen", the last page with the Einrichtungscode note), that umlauts
  show correctly and no text is cut off; then remove it in Settings, Apps, run the MSI again,
  choose Entfernen and check the page "Kalzit entfernen" with its checkbox.
- Existing certificate: install with your own PEM certificate and key, open Kalzit over HTTPS,
  then save a renewed certificate over the files and check that the browser shows it within a
  minute.
- Code signing: check the publisher shown by the installer and in the properties of `kalzit.exe`
  (Digital Signatures); for an unsigned release, check the note in the release notes.
- Prepared folders: create `C:\ProgramData\Kalzit` as a standard user before installing and check
  that the installer stops with a message; do the same with the program folder `C:\KalzitTest`.
- Plugins under the service: install the example data source plugin and check that it starts and
  answers (the service runs with SeChangeNotifyPrivilege only).
- Update through the service: with this version installed by the MSI with `SELFUPDATE=1`, install
  the next release from Verwaltung > Updates and check that the service comes back with the new
  version within a minute; install a release that fails its health check and check that Kalzit
  returns to the previous version.
- Upgrade with the next MSI over this one: settings and data stay, the service runs afterwards.
- Uninstall in Settings, Apps: the service and the firewall rule are gone, the data folder and
  `kalzit.toml` stay. Then install again, run the MSI once more, choose Remove and tick "Also
  remove the data folder": the data folder and `kalzit.toml` are gone.
- Restart Windows with Kalzit running: it starts again on its own, and the log shows a clean stop
  before the restart.
- Start Kalzit with `server.https_addr` set and without `tls.cert_file` while ACME is off: it
  refuses to start and names the missing files. Turn ACME on with HTTP-01 first, then start with
  HTTPS: it answers with the temporary self-signed certificate (or the issued one) and the log
  says so.
- Settings > Network, HTTPS and proxy: the ACME card shows "Off"; turn it on with a name that does
  not resolve and save: the error appears at Names; without accepting the terms, at the terms.
- With a real name pointing at the Windows server and port 80 or 443 forwarded to it, choose Let's
  Encrypt staging and save: the certificate card turns to Valid, and the browser shows the staging
  certificate after a reload, without a restart. Restart Kalzit: the same certificate is served at
  once.
- `kalzit secrets rotate` with Kalzit stopped, then start: the certificate is still served, the
  test mail still signs in, and Copy link of a pending invitation without mail still works.
- Add the sample SQLite source, open a dashboard with a table panel on it, open the panel's Inspect
  and check that it says "Ran in the data source; kept in the server cache for 60 seconds". Reload
  the page and check that Inspect now says "From the server cache".
- Change a value in the SQLite file with another tool, reload the dashboard and check that the old
  value stays; press Refresh and check that the new value appears and Inspect says "Refreshed in
  the data source".
- Sign in as a second user in another browser, open the same dashboard and check that Inspect says
  "Ran in the data source" for that user's first load.
- In Settings > Data sources and targets check the "Query cache now" line, press "Empty cache" and
  check that the audit log shows "Query cache emptied".
- Turn "Cache answers on the server" off for the source, save, and check that Inspect no longer
  mentions the cache.
- In PowerShell, open a connection that sends nothing and wait for Kalzit to close it:
  `$c = [Net.Sockets.TcpClient]::new("localhost", 3000); $t = Get-Date; $c.GetStream().Read([byte[]]::new(1), 0, 1); (Get-Date) - $t`
  prints 0 (the connection was closed) and a time of about 75 seconds. Meanwhile dashboards keep
  loading in the browser, and a dashboard left open with live updates stays connected afterwards.
- Start `kalzit.exe serve` in a second console with the same data folder while the service runs:
  it stops at once and says that another Kalzit server runs on the SQLite database.
- Verwaltung > Instanzen with SQLite: one server, "This server" and "Leader", "Serves one server".
- Two Windows servers on one PostgreSQL database with the same `KALZIT_SECRETS_KEY`, behind a load
  balancer: Instanzen lists both and one leader; change a setting on one and check that the other
  shows it after a reload; stop the leading service and check that the other leads within half a
  minute; Updates says that each server is updated on its own.
- ClickHouse: run `clickhouse/clickhouse-server` in Docker Desktop (or WSL), add a ClickHouse
  source with `http://localhost:8123`, press "Test connection", then in Explore run
  `SELECT number FROM numbers(5)` and check that a table with five rows appears; check that
  `INSERT INTO t VALUES (1)` is refused.
- InfluxDB 2.x: run `influxdb:2.7` with an organization, bucket and token, add an InfluxDB source
  (version v2) with the token, test it, and in Explore run a Flux query with
  `range(start: v.timeRangeStart, stop: v.timeRangeStop)`; check that a time series appears and
  that a query with `to(bucket: "x")` is refused.
- Loki: run `grafana/loki`, push a few lines, add a Loki source with `http://localhost:3100`, test
  it, and in Explore run `{app="test"}`; check that each line appears as an entry with its time and
  labels and that typing `{app="` suggests the values.
- Usage: open a dashboard with the sample SQLite source twice, wait 20 seconds, and check that
  Administration > Usage lists it under "Most viewed dashboards" with 2 views and its panel under
  "Slowest panels", and that the link opens the dashboard. Turn "Record usage" off in Settings >
  Storage and retention, open the dashboard again, and check that the views stay at 2. Stop the
  Windows service right after opening a dashboard and start it again: the view is counted.
- Open a dashboard with a hierarchy panel on the Windows server in Edge, set Layout to "ELK
  layered" and Direction to "Left to right", save, and check that the tree is drawn from left to
  right without a note that ELK could not run; reload and check that it looks the same.
- Single sign-on: run Keycloak in Docker Desktop with a realm, a confidential client `kalzit` and a
  `groups` mapper, add it under Verwaltung > Anmeldung with the redirect URI shown there, press
  "Verbindung testen" and "Testanmeldung", save, then sign out and sign in with the Keycloak
  button. Map a group to "Admin" with "IdP entscheidet" and check that the role is locked in
  Mitglieder; turn password sign-in off with yourself as break-glass admin and check that
  /anmelden/lokal still signs you in and the audit log lists it.
- SAML: on Windows Server with AD FS (or with Entra ID), add a relying party trust from Kalzit's
  metadata URL shown in Verwaltung > Anmeldung (set `server.public_url` first), with claim rules
  for E-Mail-Adresse, Name and Gruppen (`mail`, `displayName`, `memberOf` or the URIs AD FS sends);
  in Kalzit add the provider with the AD FS federation metadata URL, press "Verbindung testen" and
  "Testanmeldung", and check that the groups are listed. Save, map a group, sign out and sign in
  with the AD FS button; check the role in Mitglieder.
- LDAP: on a Windows Server domain controller (or a test domain), add the directory under
  Verwaltung > Anmeldung with `ldaps://<dc>:636`, a service account and the base DN of the users,
  press "Verbindung testen" with a test person and check that their groups are listed; save, map a
  group to "Bearbeiter", sign out and sign in with `DOMAIN\name` and the domain password, and check
  the role in Mitglieder. Disable that person in "Active Directory Users and Computers", press
  "Jetzt abgleichen" and check that the account is locked and listed under the sync.
- SCIM with Entra ID: on Kalzit running on Windows behind a public HTTPS address, create the SCIM
  token under Verwaltung > Dienstkonten, enter the endpoint and token in an Entra ID enterprise
  application (Provisioning, Automatic), test the connection, provision a test user and a group,
  then disable the user in Entra ID and check that the card counts it as deactivated and the
  account is locked.
- Service accounts: create one with the role Viewer, create a token for it, and in PowerShell run
  `Invoke-RestMethod -Headers @{ Authorization = "Bearer <token>" } https://<server>/api/orgs/<orgId>/dashboards`;
  check that the dashboards are listed. Revoke the token and check that the same call answers 401
  and that the audit log shows the refusal.
- Mail server: in Settings > Email set up a mail server you can reach from Windows (for example an
  Exchange or Microsoft 365 SMTP relay with STARTTLS on port 587), send a test mail to yourself and
  check that it arrives with umlauts intact and that the page shows the TLS version; save. Enter a
  wrong password and send a test mail: the error appears at Password, and Save stays unavailable.
- Invitation by mail: with `server.public_url` set, invite an address you can read; open the link
  in another browser, set name and password, and check that Start opens in the organization. Open
  the link again: it says the invitation was accepted already.
- Without a mail server: remove it, invite again and check that the link dialog appears and that
  "Copy link" in Open invitations copies the same link. Accept it, then set up the mail server
  again and confirm the address under Profile.
- Password reset: on the sign-in page choose Forgot password?, request a link for your account and
  for an unknown address (same answer), open the mail's link, set a new password and check that the
  other browser was signed out.
- Set `channel = "preview"` under `[updates]` in `C:\ProgramData\Kalzit\kalzit.toml`, restart the
  service and open Verwaltung > Updates: the channel list is turned off and says "In kalzit.toml
  festgelegt"; remove the line again, restart and change the channel: the notice offers
  Rückgängig.
